# How AI explained is built

This is the technical README of the site at the root of the repository (release 1.0): what every part does, how the pages are generated and what is checked. The main README is [`README.md`](../README.md).

How AI agents work, as of **October 2026**, in two ways to read the same topic, plus guides, a glossary and an about page that belong to both. The kitchen appears in two places only: the kitchen story, and the glossary, where every term shows where it sits in the kitchen.

- **The kitchen story** (`story/`) is one hand-drawn restaurant kitchen on one screen that builds itself piece by piece. A chef walks to where the next thing happens, and in the last chapter the real names are laid over the faded kitchen. Every real name opens a card, and every bold word in a caption is a link to its glossary page.
- **The technical deep dive** (`technical/`) tells the eight chapters in plain technical words with no analogy: the mechanisms, the details, dated facts and checked sources, with a map that grows beside the chapters.
- **Glossary** (`glossary/`) lists every term, 187 of them: the 21 main terms of the chapters and 166 more. The list is sorted A to Z; "By topic" is a switch in the page and in the sidebar, and the topic chips show only in that order. The cards show a term, its icon and its definition, with no counts and no chapter. Every term has the same page: *Where it sits in the kitchen* (the kitchen drawing with the spot marked, a sentence on what that part is, one button "See it in the kitchen"), *Definition*, *Related terms*, *Detailed technical description* and *References* (all of them, none folded away). The parts of a page are set off by space and a thin line, with larger headings. The box at the left edge has two parts: **On this page** (the five parts of the page, to jump to one, with the one you are reading marked) above, and **All terms** (A to Z or by topic, a filter, the list, which scrolls inside the box) below; a thin line along the lower edge of the top bar shows how far you have read, as in the guides. Wherever a text names a glossary term, it links to it. There is no worked example on the pages: the Lisbon example and "What it means for you" are not shown (they stay in the chapters of the deep dive).
- **Guides** (`guides/`) are notes on a pinboard, each with a line on what to expect. All seven (how an agent works, context engineering, building an agent, securing agents, testing and running them, what is easily confused, where to learn more) have one layout: who it is for, *what you will know afterwards*, numbered parts that each open with an *In short* line in plain words and end with the numbers of the sources they rest on, the terms used (each a glossary page) and the numbered list of sources. A thin line along the lower edge of the top bar shows how far you have read. A box at the left edge lists the parts of the guide you are in and one button, *Explore all guides*; it stays where it is while the page scrolls, and clicking a part scrolls to it smoothly (unless the visitor asked for reduced motion). Guides are technical and name no kitchen.
- **About** (`about/`) says what the site is, where the facts come from and what is stored.

The start page (`index.html`) is the first screen: two cards for the two ways in, written from the visitor's side (I am new to this, I want the full picture), and links to the guides and the glossary.

## The top bar

The same on every page, and nothing in it moves from one page to the next (the room of the scrollbar is always kept, the current place is marked without changing the width of its word, and the buttons at the right have a width of their own, so the star count arriving late moves nothing): the brand (it leads back to the start page); **the search exactly in the middle** (a field-like button that opens the search window); a **GitHub pill** with the number of stars; the **theme button**; from 1320px up the five places on a second row, centred, as pills of their own (so a long label such as "AI Agents: Technical Deep Dive" reads as one item), the current one in ink (**ELI5: AI as Kitchen Chef**, **AI Agents: Technical Deep Dive**, Guides, Glossary, About); and, below 1320px, a menu (the hamburger) that holds the same five places with a line each. (Five long labels and a centred search do not fit side by side on a laptop, hence the second row.)

- **Version.** The dropdown that lists all releases by number and codename, such as "v1.0 · Saffron" (1.0 first, marked current, then the archived alpha versions in `versions/`) is a small tab that hangs from the right edge of the bar and goes with it (the bar is sticky), from 761px up. On a phone it is the last line of the menu. `nav.js` moves the button between the two.
- **Theme.** Three choices, stepped by one button: *System* (the default; the page follows the device, also while it is open), *Light* and *Dark*. Only Light and Dark are stored, in the visitor's own browser (`ae17-theme`); no cookies. A choice an older version saved under another key is not read, so a first visit is always on System.
- **GitHub.** The pill links to the repository. `github.js` asks GitHub's public API for the number of stars (one GET, no cookies, kept for ten minutes in the tab); if the request fails, the count stays hidden. It is the one request this site makes to anyone else.
- **Search** looks through everything: the glossary, the chapters of the deep dive, the guides, the about page and the chapters of the story. The field in the bar is a button; it opens a window that floats over the page (a modal dialog with the page dimmed behind it, full screen on a phone) with the field and the hits. `/` and Ctrl or Cmd + K open it, Enter opens the first hit, the arrow keys choose, Esc, a click outside or the Esc button close it; Tab stays inside while it is open. The index (`assets/js/data/search-index.js`) is loaded the first time the window opens.
- **Hover cards.** A term anywhere (a chip, a block of the map, a word in the text, a bold word of the story) shows a short technical explanation and a link to its glossary page when the mouse rests on it or the keyboard focuses it. Touch screens get no card; a tap opens the page. Esc closes it.
- **The heading you are in stays in view.** On the long pages (a guide, a glossary term, the about page) the heading of the part you are reading sticks just under the top bar once it would scroll out, and the next part's heading pushes it away when that part arrives; a thin rule marks it while it is pinned. In the deep dive a slim bar with the chapter's number and title does the same once the chapter's own title has scrolled out. The sticking is CSS (`position: sticky`, in `pages.css` and `style.css`); `stickhead.js` marks the pinned heading, shows the chapter bar and sets `--stick-h`, which the scroll offsets use so a jump lands below the pinned heading. Printing and reduced motion are respected.
- **To the top.** On every page that is at least two windows high, a round button at the bottom right appears once you have scrolled down by most of a window (`totop.js`). It scrolls smoothly (or jumps, with reduced motion) and puts focus on the page's heading.

## The app

The site can be installed (Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers; on an iPhone or iPad: Share, Add to Home Screen) and then opens in a window of its own and without a network. `manifest.webmanifest` (name, icons, `display: standalone`), `assets/icons/` (192, 512, a maskable 512 and the home screen icon), `assets/js/pwa.js` (registers the worker, offers the browser's install prompt on the about page) and `sw.js`, the service worker. The worker sits at the root, so its scope is the whole site, but it ignores `versions/`: archived releases are never cached by it (they keep their own workers, if they have one). On install it keeps the files every page needs, the main pages and the guides; a page is fetched from the network first and the copy in the cache is used when there is no network (a page you have read is kept; for a page it does not have, the start page answers); files of the site come from the cache. The cache has the name of the build: `node scripts/build-pages.js` writes it (a hash of every file of the site) and the list of files into the block between the markers of `sw.js`, and `check-site.js` fails when it is stale. The worker makes no request to another site.

## Releases and older versions

The root of the repository is the current release, **1.0**. Every earlier version is a self-contained folder in `versions/`, unchanged. `versions/versions.js` is the one list of releases (newest first, the one with `main: true` is the root; each has a one-word herb or spice as codename, given alphabetically); `versions/version-switcher.js` and `.css` draw the dropdown in every page, root and archived alike. `scripts/versions.test.js` fails when the list, the folders and the script tags disagree, or when an old address no longer forwards. `.claude/skills/new-version/SKILL.md` describes how the next release is cut.

## How it is built

No build step apart from the generated files, no frameworks, no requests to anywhere else except the star count; everything is in the repository root (older releases are archived in `versions/`).

## Phones and tablets

Four reviewers tested every page type in Chromium at 280 to 1920px wide, in portrait and landscape, with touch, light and dark, and reduced motion, and the fixes are in the files above. In short: touch targets are at least 40px (chips, source numbers, list rows, the map's pieces, the story's chapter chips and scrubber); inputs are 16px so iOS does not zoom; nothing scrolls sideways (guide tables, the pinboard of notes, long terms at 280px); the glossary box above a term opens, scrolls inside and closes after a jump; the story's camera keeps speech bubbles and the chef in view and the caption keeps its height, and the names view zooms to each chapter's names on a phone; the top bar, the search, the version tab and the to-top button keep clear of the notch and of the content; the version button moves into the menu on a phone and on a landscape phone. Known limits: on a very small landscape phone (about 375px high) two speech bubbles can touch, and on a 320px-wide phone an open name card covers the label that was tapped.

## Check it

`node scripts/check-site.js` runs the checks: the content (word budgets, links, sources), the story (steps, things, captions, names), the bridge (every name is a term with a glossary page, every term is named over the kitchen, every bold word has a bridge), the kitchen kept where it belongs (only the story and the glossary pages load a kitchen script, and the deep dive, the guides and the hover texts name no kitchen), the glossary (every term has an icon, a kitchen sentence, a detailed description, 2 to 4 references and links that resolve), the guides (one shape: sections with an In short line, every source cited, every address in the text a source, terms linked), the top bar (the same on every page), the app (manifest, icons, a worker that names no outside address), the generated files up to date, nothing requested from outside the folder (but the star count in `github.js`) and nothing that refers to another version. `node --test scripts/*.test.js` runs its tests.

## Accessibility and privacy

Keyboard only works everywhere (`/` or Ctrl/Cmd+K for the search, `Esc` closes the search, the menu, the hover card and the story's card; story: arrow keys, Space, `P`, `S`); reduced motion stops the walking, the drawing, the card's fade and the smooth scrolling and flash in the guides; light and dark themes follow the system by default (System), or the choice in the theme button. No cookies, no analytics. The only request to anyone else is the star count of the GitHub pill (GitHub's public API, no cookies). The site's own files and the pages you have read are kept in the browser's cache so that it works offline (nothing leaves the device). Stored in the visitor's own browser: the theme (`ae17-theme`: only Light or Dark; System is no stored value), the story's speed (`ae-speed`), the glossary order (`ae15-order`, A to Z or by topic) and the build checklist.
